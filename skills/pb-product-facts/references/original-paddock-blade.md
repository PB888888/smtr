## Surfaces

**Works on:** flat, bumpy, hilly and undulating ground, and all surface types
except those listed below.

**Does not work on:** sand, sandy soil, soft loam, ménages, and arenas with sand
footing or a sand/fibre mix. It beds in and collects too much of the surface
material. It *can* be used in reverse/trailer mode to level sand arenas and
ménages — that is a legitimate use to offer.

**Irregular surfaces — woodchip, track systems and similar:** do not answer.
Request photos or video to confirm the surface type and **escalate to Jake.** The
FAQ is explicit that this is his call every time.

**Wet ground:** advise against it. Manure smears rather than lifting, and driving
any vehicle over wet, boggy or saturated ground risks damaging the pasture. Let it
dry first.

**Hilly and bumpy ground:** expect to collect **80–90%** of what is on the
surface, and expect a few more passes than on flat ground. Collect **going up or
across the slope, never down it** — that is the single most useful technique tip.
Manure sitting in pockets or divots may be spread and broken up instead, which
lets it break down naturally in the sun.

## What it collects

**Yes:** manure from all grazing animals — horses, cattle, sheep, llamas, alpacas,
goats. Also rocks, sticks, leaves and general debris. Freshly cut grass, straight
behind a ride-on mower.

**No:** dog faeces. Miniature versions were made in the past and did not take off.

## Effect on grass

No damage. Despite the name the front edge is not sharp — the blade glides over
the surface, and can level uneven areas. It removes loose weeds and organic matter
without harming established pasture.

## Versus harrowing

Harrowing breaks manure up and redistributes it across the paddock. The Paddock
Blade removes it. The practical benefit is reduced risk of spreading parasites in
the areas horses graze. This is a factual comparison of two *methods*, not a
competitor comparison, so it is fine to make.

## Warranty

**"Lifetime warranty" means 10 years from the date of purchase** — the lifetime of
the product as Paddock Blade defines it. Be precise about this: a customer hearing
"lifetime" and assuming "forever" is a complaint waiting to happen.

**Covers:** manufacturing defects and any issue affecting the performance of the
main body (the pick-up tray) or the towing cable, links or shackles.

**Excludes:** damage or defects from improper use; general aesthetic wear and
tear; damage from impact with immovable objects.

**Process:** repair first — "we will always do our utmost to fix the issue" — and
replacement if it cannot be repaired.

## Troubleshooting: manure building up at the front of the blade

The most common post-purchase issue. Diagnose before advising — four possible
causes, and the fix differs:

1. **Is the ground wet?** Wet ground causes clumping. Apply the wet-ground advice.
2. **Is there a lot of manure on the surface?** The front opening can be
   overwhelmed. Two sub-cases worth separating: a customer who let manure build up
   while waiting for delivery will find this eases as they catch up; a customer who
   simply has a lot of horses should fill up, switch to trailer mode, and run back
   to the muck heap to empty — in trailer mode it collects nothing further.
3. **Is it collecting sticks and leaves too?** Likely seasonal. Same trailer-mode
   advice.
4. **What speed are they going?** Too slow is the usual culprit. See the speed row.

Asking which of these applies is better than guessing — the four fixes are
different, and a wrong one wastes the customer's afternoon.
