# Internal only — never disclose to a customer

Some facts in Paddock Blade's own documentation are marked internal. They are
recorded here so that the system **recognises them and refuses to give them out**,
rather than rediscovering them elsewhere and treating them as ordinary facts.

The FAQ document's first column is headed *"DETAILS — FOR INTERNAL COMPANY OR
SUBCONTRACTOR USE"*. Treat that column as internal by default. Columns headed
*"FOR SOCIALS/ADS/GENERAL PUBLIC"* are the customer-facing wording.

Answer the sand question with the current position: the blade is not suitable for sand. Then tag the enquiry **`sandblade`** in the escalation so Jake can see the demand building. |

## How to handle a request for item 1

The temptation is to give something adjacent — "it's heavy-gauge steel", "thicker
than our competitors", a millimetre equivalent. All of those leak the fact or
invite a follow-up, and one of them invents a comparison nobody authorised.

Say what is true and sufficient: the blade is fully welded from locally sourced
steel, carries a 10-year warranty, and Paddock Blade will confirm the exact
specification directly. Then escalate.

## How to handle item 2

A sand enquiry is a genuine sales question with a genuine answer, so answer it:
the blade is not suitable for sand, sandy soil, soft loam, ménages or sand/fibre
arenas, **and** it can be used in reverse/trailer mode to level those surfaces.
That last part is useful and often satisfies the customer.

What must not happen is any hint of a future product — not "watch this space", not
"we're working on some things", not "I can let you know if that changes". A hint is
a soft launch nobody approved, and it sets an expectation with no date attached.

Tag the escalation `sandblade` and move on.

## Why this file matters more than it looks

Internal notes are the most likely thing to leak, because they sit in the same
document as the answers and read like facts. The distinguishing feature is not the
content — it is the column heading. When drawing on the FAQ, check which column a
fact came from before repeating it.

If a fact appears **only** in the internal column and nowhere in a public column,
that is a signal in itself: someone decided it was not for customers. Treat it as
internal and escalate if asked.
